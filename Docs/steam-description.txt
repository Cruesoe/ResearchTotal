[b]Set how many research points the whole tech tree should cost, and every project scales to add up to that total.[/b]

It's hard to scale up a tech tree, so I looked at it the other way: what if it was about the total, not the cost of each tech? Research speed is fairly consistent once you get going, so setting a total really says how long you want it to take to reach the end of the tree. Is a 1 million point game hard or easy? Is 5 million too much?

[h2]Features[/h2]
[list]
[*]All research projects are adjusted so the whole tree costs the total you set, give or take small rounding.
[*]Techs you start with are not affected; their cost is deducted from the total and the rest is spread across the tree.
[*]Each tech keeps its vanilla proportions, and era weights decide how much of the budget each era gets.
[*]Vanilla's tech-level penalty still applies, so researching ahead of your colony's tech level takes longer.
[*]If a mod raises your faction's tech level (for example Tech Advancing), finished techs keep their cost and unfinished ones are recosted so the remaining budget still adds up.
[*]Anomaly dark study has its own knowledge target, since it uses knowledge from studying entities rather than bench research. Vanilla Anomaly is about 1,280 knowledge for the whole tree.
[*]Vanilla Gravship Expanded's gravtech has its own target, rounding and era weights when that mod is installed, since it uses gravdata instead of the research bench.
[/list]

[h2]Settings[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Standard Research[/b]: the research point target for the whole tree, rounding, and era weights. Later eras get more by default (Neolithic 1.0, Medieval 1.15, Industrial 1.5, Spacer 2.0, Ultra 2.5) because research gets faster as you unlock better benches.
[*][b]Anomaly[/b]: a knowledge target, rounding, and separate Basic and Advanced weights. The tech-level penalty doesn't apply.
[*][b]Vanilla Gravship Expanded[/b]: a gravtech target, rounding and era weights, used when that mod is installed.
[/list]

[h2]Compatibility[/h2]
Not compatible with Research Inflation, because both change research costs.
A new save is recommended. Adding it to an existing save won't break anything, but some costs may look odd.
